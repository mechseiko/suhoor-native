Conditions for Fasting

1. Islam
The first requirement is to be Muslim. Therefore, those who do not believe in Islam are not obliged to fast.

2. Puberty
Second, the mandatory requirement for fasting is for those who are of puberty. Small children are not obliged to observe obligatory fasts, however, their parents are obliged to train them to observe fasts from the age of seven.

3. Be sensible
The next condition is to be sensible. What this means is that only sensible people are obliged to fast. According to the consensus of the ulama, mentally ill people are people who are not rational, so they are not obliged to fast.

4. Healthy
Sick people do not have the obligation to carry out mandatory fasting such as Ramadan. However, he had to replace it another day. This is in accordance with the word of Allah in surah Al Baqarah verse 185,

وَمَنْ كَانَ مَرِيْضًا اَوْ عَلٰى سَفَرٍ فَعِدَّةٌ مِّنْ اَيَّامٍ اَخَرَ

Meaning: "...And whoever is sick or on a journey (he does not fast), then (must make up for it), as many days as he missed, on other days..."

5. Capable
Furthermore, the condition for fasting is being able. The meaning is that it is mandatory for those who do it. For those who are physically weak due to age or it is not possible to fast, they are not obliged to fast. This is also in accordance with the word of Allah in Surah Al Baqarah verse 184

وَعَلَى الَّذِيْنَ يُطِيْقُوْنَهٗ فِدْيَةٌ طَعَامُ مِسْكِيْنٍ

Meaning: "...And for people who carry it hard, they are obliged to pay fidyah, namely feeding the poor..."

6. Traveling
Based on Al Baqarah verse 185 above (on point 4) it is permissible for those who are on journey or travelling not to fast. However, according to the opinion of scholars, not all types of travel allow someone not to fast. Some conditions are:

- The journey undertaken must cover a distance that permits the shortening of prayers (qashar salat). According to the measurement of the Umayyad Dynasty, a person is considered a traveler if they have covered four buruds, equivalent to 40 miles, or 48 miles according to the measurement of the Hashemite. However, in the book Al-Fiqh Al-Manhaji, Dr. Musthofa Al-Khin and his colleagues explicitly convert this measurement into kilometers, amounting to 81 kilometers.
- The journey undertaken should be permissible, not for the purpose of committing sin.
- The journey should commence during the night, and before the break of dawn (subuh). By this time, the traveler must have crossed the boundaries of their residential area, within the context of Indonesia, the limits of the neighborhood or subdistrict. This aligns with the teachings of KH. Subhan Ma'mun, the Custodian of Assalafiyah Islamic Boarding School in Luwungragi, Brebes, and the Chief Advisory Board of Nahdlatul Ulama (PBNU), as conveyed during a study of the interpretation of Al-Munir at the Islamic Center Brebes.
- If the journey begins after the break of dawn, the individual cannot be considered a traveler, and they are not allowed to break their fast. They must observe a full day of fasting.
- A traveler, meeting the conditions mentioned above, is permitted to break their fast and invalidate their fasting during the morning iftar.
- A traveler who has settled in a particular place is prohibited from breaking their fast or abstaining from fasting.

A traveler is also allowed to continue fasting if capable. An individual is permitted to break or not observe fasting if the purpose of the journey is for worship. However, if the journey is undertaken for sinful activities, there is no leniency for them.

7. Chaste and Menstruation and Postpartum
According to the consensus of the ulama, women who are menstruating or postpartum are not required to fast. The basis is based on the hadith narrated by Aisyah that:

"We (menstruating or postpartum women) are ordered to make up for fasting and are not ordered to make up for prayers."

Fasting Supplication

Niyyat to Fast on Monday

نَوَيْتُ صَوْمَ غَدٍ فِي يَوْمِ الْإِثْنَيْنِ سُنَّةً لِلّٰهِ تَعَالَى

Nawaitu sauma gadin fi yaumil-isnaini sunnatal lillāhi ta'ālā.

Meaning:
"I intend to fast on sunnah Mondays because of Allah Ta'ala."

Niyyat to Fast on Thursday

نَوَيْثُ صَوْمَ غَدٍ فِي يَوْمِ الْخَمِيسِ سُنَّةً لِلّٰهِ تَعَالَى

Nawaitu sauma gadin fi yaumil-khamisi sunnatan lillāhi ta'ālā.

Meaning:
"I intend to fast on sunnah Thursdays because of Allah Ta'ala."

Sunnah Practices

1. Having Sahur (Pre-Dawn Meal):
Engaging in Sahur is a rewarding practice in the month of Ramadan, even if it's just a sip of water, as advised by the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him).

تَسَحَّرُوا فَإِنَّ فِي السَّحُورِ بَرَكَةً

"Partake in Sahur, for indeed, there is blessing in Sahur." (Narrated by al-Bukhari)

The recommended time for Sahur is towards the end, as mentioned by the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him):

لَا تَزَالُ أُمَّتِي بِخَيْرٍ مَا أَخَّرُوا السَّحُورَ وَعَجَّلُوا الفِطْرَ

"My Ummah will remain on the right path as long as they hasten in the breaking of the fast and delay having the pre-dawn meal (Sahur)." (Narrated by Ahmad)

2. Hastening to Break the Fast:
Another practice is to hasten in breaking the fast before the Maghrib prayer. When breaking the fast initially, it is Sunnah to do so with dates. If not available, then with water, as per the saying of the Prophet:

إِذَا كَانَ أَحَدُكُمْ صَائِمًا، فَلْيُفْطِرْ عَلَى التَّمْرِ، فَإِنْ لَمْ يَجِدِ التَّمْرَ، فَعَلَى الْمَاءِ فَإِنَّ الْمَاءَ طَهُورٌ

"If one of you is fasting, let him break his fast with dates. If he does not have dates, then with water, for indeed, water is purifying." (Narrated by Abu Dawud)

3. Reciting the Du'a for Breaking the Fast:
When breaking the fast, it is recommended to recite the du'a for breaking the fast as a gesture of gratitude for the sustenance provided by Allah after enduring hunger and thirst. Breaking the fast is also an opportune moment for supplication, as indicated in the following hadith:

ثَلَاثَةٌ لَا تُرَدُّ دَعْوَتُهُمْ: الْإِمَامُ الْعَادِلُ وَالصَّائِمُ حِينَ يُفْطِرُ وَدَعْوَةُ الْمَظْلُومِ يَرْفَعُهَا فَوْقَ الْغَمَامِ وَتُفَتَّحُ لَهَا أَبْوَابُ السَّمَاءِ

"Three supplications are never rejected: the supplication of a just leader, the supplication of a fasting person when he breaks his fast, and the supplication of the oppressed that rises above the clouds and the gates of Heaven are opened for it." (Narrated by Tirmidhi)

The du'a for breaking the fast is:

اللَّهُمَّ لَكَ صُمْتُ وَبِكَ آمَنْتُ وَعَلَى رِزْقِكَ أَفْطَرْتُ بِرَحْمَتِكَ يَا أَرْحَمَ الرَّاحِمِينَ

"Allahumma lakasumtu wabika aamantu wa'alarizqika afthortu birohmatika ya ar-rahamarrahimin"
Translation: "O Allah, for You, I have fasted, and in You, I believe, and with Your sustenance, I break my fast, O Most Merciful of the merciful."

4. Guarding the Tongue:
It is crucial to restrain the tongue from useless or, worse, forbidden speech, such as lying and backbiting, as these actions can nullify the rewards of fasting.

5. Increasing Acts of Charity:
Those who fast should strive to increase acts of charity towards others, especially by providing food or drinks for breaking the fast. The one who provides sustenance for a fasting person receives a reward equivalent to the fasting person without diminishing the fasting person's reward.

مَنْ فَطَّرَ صَائِمًا، كُتِبَ لَهُ مِثْلُ أَجْرِهِ، إِلَّا أَنَّهُ لَا يَنْقُصُ مِنْ أَجْرِ الصَّائِمِ شَيْءٌ

"Whoever feeds a fasting person will have a reward like that of the fasting person, without any reduction in his reward." (Narrated by Ahmad)

6. Reading the Quran:
Fasting time is opportune for reading and studying the Quran. As conveyed by the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) to Abdullah bin Amru, "Read (complete) the Quran once a month." (Narrated by Bukhari)

Fasting Invalidator

1. Intentional ingestion of substances:
During fasting, a true Muslim should not intentionally introduce anything into the body. This means nothing should enter the body through any opening, such as the mouth, nose, or even ears. Anything intentionally ingested, whether it be food, drink, medicine, or otherwise, will invalidate the fast unless it enters unintentionally. However, there are specific limits set in Islam. For instance, if something enters the mouth, it should not go beyond the throat. If it enters the nose, it should not reach the gills, which are parallel to the eyes and ears, and should not go beyond what is not visible to the eye.

2. Medication administered anally:
Medical treatments administered through the rectum, either through the back or front opening, can also break the fast. This type of treatment is commonly used for hemorrhoid patients.
Additionally, individuals who are ill and need to use a urinary catheter cannot continue fasting, as it invalidates their obligatory worship.

3. Intentional vomiting:
If someone vomits suddenly and unintentionally, the fast remains valid, provided that none of the vomited matter is swallowed. However, intentional vomiting that involves swallowing part of the vomit renders the fast invalid.

4. Emission of semen upon skin contact:
Apart from vomiting, the release of sperm or semen after skin contact, even without sexual intercourse, also nullifies the fast. This can occur without engaging in sexual activity. However, if semen is discharged due to a wet dream (ihtilam) or in an unconscious state, the fast can still be continued.

5. Sexual relations:
Engaging in sexual intercourse during fasting not only invalidates the fast but also incurs sin. The penalty includes fasting consecutively for two months or providing a meal to 60 poor individuals, equivalent to 0.6 kilograms of rice each.

6. Menstruation:
Menstruation or menstrual bleeding can also break the fast due to the discharge of blood from the body. Similarly, if a woman is experiencing postpartum bleeding (nifas), her fast is considered invalid. In addition to being invalid, women menstruating or in postpartum bleeding must make up for the missed fasting days.

7. Mental disorders:
Mental disorders (junun) that arise during fasting can also invalidate the fast. Those experiencing this condition must make up for the missed fasting days once they have recovered.

8. Leaving Islam:
Muslims who suddenly decide to leave Islam, known as apostasy (murtad), also nullify their fast. This includes acts such as associating partners with Allah or rejecting the laws of Islamic Sharia.

In such cases, Muslims must make up for the missed fasts and promptly recite the declaration of faith (shahadah) again.

Benefits of Fasting

Here are several general benefits of fasting, quoting from the book "Mukjizat Puasa" (Miracles of Fasting) and the book "Inilah Alasan Rasulullah SAW Menganjurkan Puasa Sunnah" (This is the Reason the Prophet Muhammad SAW Recommends Sunnah Fasting) by Amirulloh Syarbini & Iis Nuraeni Afgandi:

1. Self-Purification:
Fasting is an act of obedience to what Allah SWT has prescribed, and it involves abstaining from His prohibitions. Through the effort to perfect this devotion, a purification within oneself is achieved. It's noteworthy that someone could choose not to fast by discreetly eating, drinking, or engaging in activities that invalidate the fast. Since fasting is an act of worship known only to Allah SWT and the individual, people choose to restrain all these desires with the aim of seeking Allah's pleasure.

2. Attaining the Rank of Taqwa (Piety):
Fasting has the potential to elevate the practitioner to the level of piety. Ibn Qayyim stated, "Fasting provides extraordinary protection for the body's limbs and inner strength, shielding them from external elements that can harm and dominate them. Additionally, it purges the body of harmful elements, controls the health of the heart and limbs, and instills the habit of restraining desires. This is the greatest path to piety." Allah SWT also states in Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 183: "O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may become righteous."

3. Self-Education Process:
Through fasting, an individual is trained to cultivate patience, strive in the path of Allah SWT, and make an effort to control their habits. Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) likened fasting to a shield: "Fasting is a shield, so do not speak obscenely or behave ignorantly. If someone attacks or insults you, say, 'I am fasting,' twice." (Narrated by Bukhari & Muslim)

4. Training to Control Desires:
It is said that fasting has a significant influence in restraining one's desires, especially when done with the intention of seeking Allah's pleasure. As Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) recommended to young people who are unable to marry to fast, as it can suppress their sexual desires if the intention is sincere.

5. Teaching Gratitude for Blessings:
By reflecting, one only truly appreciates the feeling of fullness when hungry. Similarly, when thirst is quenched, the enjoyment is realized in times of thirst. Likewise, through fasting, individuals are reminded to appreciate blessings, even if seemingly insignificant.

6. Social Wisdom:
By abstaining from various desires throughout the day, fasting nurtures compassion, sharing, and empathy towards fellow human beings, especially the poor. Ibn Qayyim mentioned, "Fasting serves as a reminder of the hunger experienced by the poor." Ibn Hammam also stated, "Indeed, those who fast, when they experience hunger throughout the day, quickly feel moved to show compassion."

7. Maintaining Body Health:
Numerous studies by experts indicate that individuals who fast may initially feel lethargic, sleepy, or dizzy, but the body adapts over time. The body becomes fresh and healthy as toxins are naturally cleansed. Several doctors state that fasting is effective in healing various illnesses and alleviating pain. Conditions such as gastric problems, inflammation of the colon, shortness of breath, vascular diseases, eye disorders, skin diseases, impotence, headaches, and others can be positively impacted by fasting.

Things Diminish Rewards

1. Lying or Deceit:
Fasting is one of the essential acts of worship in Islam. However, there are actions that can diminish the rewards of fasting. Some things that can reduce the rewards of fasting include:

Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said, "Whoever does not abandon false speech and acting upon it, Allah has no need for him to abandon his food and drink." (Narrated by Bukhari-Muslim)

2. Using Abusive Language and Speaking Harshly:
Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) emphasized that fasting is not just refraining from food and drink but also abstaining from useless words and actions. If someone mocks or behaves harshly towards you, say, 'I am fasting.' (Narrated by Bukhari)

3. Gossiping
Actions that can nullify the rewards of fasting, such as gossiping, spreading discord, and lying, as stated by Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) in the following hadith:

خمس يُفطِرن الصائِمَ: الغِيبةُ، والنَّميمةُ، والكِذبُ، والنَّظرُ بِالشَّهوةِ، واليمينُ الكاذِبةُ

"Five things break the fast: gossiping, spreading discord, lying, looking with lust, and false oaths." (Narrated by Ad-Dailami)

Referring to the interpretation of Surah Al-Hujurat 49:12, gossiping about others is likened to eating the flesh of one's dead brother.

4. Excessive Sleep:
While there is a hadith stating, 'The sleep of a fasting person is worship, their silence is glorification, their prayers are answered, and their rewards are multiplied,' it is mentioned by three narrators that this hadith is weak, as quoted by IslamQA.

Moreover, spending the entire fasting period sleeping contradicts the actions of the Prophet. Even though fasting, one should not change their work or regular activities. Sleep is allowed but in moderation—neither too little nor excessive, as both are equally harmful to health. So, continue with regular activities even while fasting.

5. Excessive Eating during Suhoor and Iftar:
Fasting also trains us to control ourselves and discipline our bodies regarding all the food and drinks consumed. Prophet Muhammad taught not to overindulge and to control oneself in all aspects of life, prohibiting us from harming ourselves, others, or the environment.

Consuming excess food only has adverse effects on health. So, even though you have been hungry all day, do not seek revenge during iftar or suhoor. Eat in moderation. Additionally, consume nutritious food, not fast food and high-calorie items.

6. Showing Off (Riya'):
When someone fasts with the intention of seeking praise from others or feeling that their fasting is better than that of others, it can nullify the rewards of fasting. Furthermore, showing off is considered an act of associating partners with Allah (shirk). Prophet Muhammad said:

ومَنْ صَامَ يُرائِي فقَدْ أشرَكَ

"Whoever fasts while showing off, he has committed shirk (associating partners with Allah)." (Narrated by Ahmad, Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, and Thabrani)

7. Breaking the Fast with Haram (Forbidden) Food:
Breaking the fast with something forbidden can nullify its rewards. Forbidden food includes stolen items or anything deemed impure in Islam. Besides eliminating the rewards of fasting, consuming forbidden food can also make a person lazy in worship, making it easy to abandon it. (Habib Zain bin Smith, al-Fawaidul Mukhtarah li Saliki Tariqil Akhirah, p. 587)

8. Hugging and Kissing Spouse:
Physical affection such as hugging or kissing between married couples during fasting is considered to undermine the essence of worship and may encourage the emergence of lust.
While not prohibited, kissing or hugging one's spouse while fasting is disliked (makruh). Couples should refrain from this until after breaking the fast.
